\section{Realization of elementary defects}\label{sec:app-er}

The finite spectra above extend to infinite families. The key step is to
compare exact-rank counting bounds while holding the desired defect fixed.

\begin{theorem}[elementary realization]\label{thm:er}
For every fixed $a\ge1$, all sufficiently large $h$ admit an $\STS(2^{h}-1)$
with $\Delt\cong(\ZZ/2)^{a}$; for every fixed $c\ge1$, all sufficiently large
$h$ admit an $\STS(3^{h})$ with $\Delt\cong(\ZZ/3)^{c}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We prove positivity of the exact-rank difference bounds of
Jungnickel--Tonchev~\cite[Theorems 4.4 and 4.12]{JungnickelTonchev} in the
moving-parameter regime needed here. Their parameter $n$ is denoted by $h$
in this appendix, to distinguish it from $v-1$.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{1. Counting inputs.} Let $N_1(q)$ count Steiner triple systems on a fixed
$q$-point set, $N_2(q)$ count one-factorizations of the labeled $K_q$, and
$N_3(q)$ count transversal designs on three specified groups of size $q$
(equivalently, labeled Latin squares). The required asymptotics are
\begin{align*}
\log N_1(q)&=\tfrac16q^2(\log q-2+o(1)) &&(q\equiv1,3\pmod6),\\
\log N_2(q)&=\tfrac12q^2(\log q-2+o(1)) &&(q\text{ even}),\\
\log N_3(q)&=q^2(\log q-2+o(1)).
\end{align*}
These are Keevash's enumeration theorem~\cite[Theorem 2.2]{KeevashCounting},
the complete-graph case of~\cite[Theorem 1.2]{FerberJainSudakov} and its
matching upper bound, and the permanent-bound Latin-square estimate recalled
in the introduction of the latter paper. Ordering factors changes logarithms
by at most $O(q\log q)$; exact formulas below use Jungnickel--Tonchev's
conventions. Also $\log(q!)=O(q\log q)$.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{2. Ternary case.} Fix $c\ge1$, put $t=h-c$, $T=3^t$, and $M=3^c$.
For $h\ge c+2$, the source condition $2\le t\le h-1$ holds. Set
\[
F_3(T,M)=N_1(T)^M N_3(T)^{M(M-1)/6}.
\]
The number of isomorphism classes of exact rank $3^h-h-1+t=v-1-c$
is at least $A_3-B_3$, where
\[
A_3=\frac{F_3(T,M)}{(T!)^M|\operatorname{AGL}(c,3)|},\qquad
B_3=\frac{F_3(T/3,3M)}{((T/3)!)^{3M-c-2}}.
\]
All factorial exponents and group orders are fixed as $h\to\infty$.
Consequently
\begin{align*}
\log A_3&=\tfrac16M^2T^2(\log T-2+o(1)),\\
\log B_3&=\tfrac16M^2T^2(\log T-\log3-2+o(1)),\\
\log(A_3/B_3)&=\tfrac16M^2T^2\log3+o(T^2)>0
\end{align*}
for all sufficiently large $h$. This proves existence at the exact rank,
not merely at a rank bounded above by it.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{3. Binary case.} Fix $a\ge1$, put $t=h-a$, $S=2^t$, $M=2^a-1$,
and $Q=M+1=2^a$. For $h\ge a+2$ the same source condition holds. Define
\[
F_2(S,M)=N_1(S-1)\bigl(N_2(S)(S-1)!\bigr)^M
N_3(S)^{M(M-1)/6}.
\]
The exact-rank-$2^h-h-1+t=v-a$ count is at least $A_2-B_2$, with
\[
A_2=\frac{F_2(S,M)}{(S-1)!(S!)^M|\operatorname{PGL}(a,2)|},\qquad
B_2=\frac{F_2(S/2,2M+1)}{((S/2)!)^{2M-a-1}}.
\]
The coefficient of $S^2(\log S-2)$ in $\log A_2$ is
$1/6+M/2+M(M-1)/6=Q^2/6$. Replacing $S$ by $S-1$ in $N_1$ and
including the factorials changes this by only $o(S^2)$. For $B_2$ the group
size is $S/2$ and the corresponding coefficient is $(2Q)^2/6$. Thus
\begin{align*}
\log A_2&=\tfrac16Q^2S^2(\log S-2+o(1)),\\
\log B_2&=\tfrac16Q^2S^2(\log S-\log2-2+o(1)),\\
\log(A_2/B_2)&=\tfrac16Q^2S^2\log2+o(S^2)>0
\end{align*}
eventually, proving the required exact-rank existence.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{4. Conversion to the selected group.} For $v=2^h-1$, the annihilator
$d=(v-3)/2$ has $v_2(d)=1$ for $h\ge3$. Exact binary rank $v-a$ gives
$a$ binary generators of $\Delt$ (the augmentation quotient has odd order).
The primary purity in Appendix~\ref{sec:app-sts} excludes other primes,
so $\Delt\cong(\ZZ/2)^a$. For $v=3^h$, $v_3(d)=1$ for $h\ge2$.
By Proposition~\ref{prop:split},
$\coker(A)\cong\ZZ/3\oplus\Delt$; thus rank $v-1-c$, whose incidence
deficiency is $c+1$, gives exactly $c$ ternary generators of $\Delt$.
Purity and valuation one yield $\Delt\cong(\ZZ/3)^c$.
\end{proof}

The threshold in $h$ is not made effective here. The constructions and
exact-rank bounds are imported; the displayed moving-parameter comparison
and its translation to the selected quotient are the deductions supplied
in this appendix. No non-elementary or mixed-primary realization is claimed.
