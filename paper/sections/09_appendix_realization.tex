\section{Realization of elementary defects}\label{sec:app-er}

\begin{theorem}[elementary realization]\label{thm:er}
For every fixed $a\ge1$, all sufficiently large $n$ admit an $\STS(2^{n}-1)$
with $\Delt\cong(\ZZ/2)^{a}$; for every fixed $b\ge1$, all sufficiently large
$n$ admit an $\STS(3^{n})$ with $\Delt\cong(\ZZ/3)^{b}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:sts15}, $\Delt=(\ZZ/2)^{a}$ is exactly a binary rank
deficiency $a$ of the incidence matrix, and $(\ZZ/3)^{b}$ a ternary deficiency
$b$; realizing the group is realizing the rank. Jungnickel and
Tonchev~\cite{JungnickelTonchev} enumerate the Steiner triple systems with
classical parameters and prescribed $2$-rank (resp.\ $3$-rank), and show that for
each fixed deficiency the number of such systems grows without bound as the order
increases. Hence, for every fixed $a$ (resp.\ $b$), systems of exactly that
deficiency exist for all sufficiently large $n$; valuation one, the $\STS$
primary purity of Theorem~\ref{thm:sts15}, and the ternary augmentation splitting
of Proposition~\ref{prop:split} then convert the deficiency into the stated
homocyclic group. The prescribed-rank carriers and their counts are
Jungnickel--Tonchev's; only the translation into $\Delt$ is added here.
\end{proof}
